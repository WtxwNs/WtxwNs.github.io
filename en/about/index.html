About

From robotic systems to learning theory

An engineering background keeps me interested in how algorithms behave under noise, feedback, and limited computation.

I am an undergraduate at the School of Future Technology, Southeast University. My early training came from robotics, control, and embedded systems. Sensing noise, contact feedback, and limited compute in physical systems gradually led me to more basic questions: why a learning algorithm is stable, when it can adapt to change, and how it should allocate computation.

My current work spans reinforcement learning, multi-agent learning, and efficient language models. I usually begin by identifying the mathematical structure that controls behavior, then ask whether it yields a clearer boundary or a more effective algorithm.

This site also holds paper notes, research judgments in progress, and lessons from engineering work.
